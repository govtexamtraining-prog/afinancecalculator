Refinance calculator

Mortgage refinance calculator comparing your current payment with a new rate and term. Closing costs are the amount you enter. It is not a lender quote.

Worked example

A lower rate on the same balance reduces the payment. If closing costs are $6,000 and the payment falls by $200 a month, the costs are covered in 30 months. After that, the lower payment is the gain. If the new payment is higher, there is no break-even from payment savings.

How this figure is calculated

The current payment and the new payment are each a standard monthly principal-and-interest calculation on the balance, rate, and term you enter. The monthly savings are the old payment minus the new payment. Break-even months are the closing costs you enter divided by that monthly savings, when the new payment is lower.

What this page leaves out

The result is not a lender quote. Cash-out, points bought with a higher fee, mortgage insurance, and prepayment penalties are only present if you have already put them into the balance or the closing-cost field.

Questions

When should I use a refinance calculator?

Use it when you have a current balance, rate, and term and want to compare a new rate and term after closing costs. It shows payment change and break-even months, not a lender offer.

Does this mortgage refinance calculator include cash-out?

Only if you raise the balance to include cash-out. Points and penalties count only when you put them in the closing-cost or balance fields.

How is refinance break-even calculated?

Closing costs divided by the monthly payment savings, when the new payment is lower. If the new payment is higher, there is no break-even from payment savings alone.
