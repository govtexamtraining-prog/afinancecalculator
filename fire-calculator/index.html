FIRE calculator

FIRE calculator and retirement calculator estimate for years until the portfolio covers spending at the growth rate you enter, using a 4% withdrawal target. Not a historical Monte Carlo and not a guarantee. Coast FIRE searches can use this as a simple target estimate.

Worked example

Spending of $60,000 a year at a 4% withdrawal rate sets a target of $1,500,000. If savings are already at that target, the years to get there are zero. If they are not, the years depend on the starting balance, the yearly contribution, and the growth rate, which defaults to 7% when that field is empty.

How this figure is calculated

The target portfolio is annual spending divided by the withdrawal rate. At 4%, the target is spending times 25. Each year the current savings grow by the growth rate you enter, and then the annual savings amount is added. The page counts years until the balance reaches the target, and it stops at 60 years.

What this page leaves out

A 4% withdrawal is a planning target, not a guarantee that the portfolio survives. Social Security, CPP, a pension, and taxes are not subtracted from the spending figure unless you have already done that yourself.

Questions

What is a FIRE calculator?

It estimates years until savings cover annual spending at a withdrawal rate such as 4%. This page grows savings each year and stops when the FIRE number is reached, or at 60 years.

Does this include a Coast FIRE calculator?

Not as a separate Coast FIRE mode. You can treat the FIRE number as a target and stop new contributions mentally once invested assets would grow to that target by retirement age. It is not a historical Monte Carlo like FIRECalc.

Is this a retirement calculator guarantee?

No. The 4% rule is a planning target. Markets, taxes, and pensions are not simulated here.
