🇺🇸 FDIC Deposit Insurance ($250,000 USD)

The Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation (FDIC) is an independent U.S. government agency. Deposit insurance covers up to $250,000 USD per depositor, per insured bank, for each ownership category:

• Individual Accounts: Insured up to $250,000.

• Joint Accounts: Insured up to $500,000 ($250k per co-owner).

• Revocable Trust Accounts: Up to $1,250,000 (5 beneficiaries).

• Cash Sweep Networks (MaxSafe, IntraFi): Multi-bank sweeps expanding FDIC coverage up to $5,000,000+.

The Canada Deposit Insurance Corporation (CDIC) is a federal Crown corporation protecting deposits up to $100,000 CAD per insured category, per member institution:

• Registered Accounts: Separate $100k coverage for RRSPs, TFSAs, RRIFs, FHSAs, and RESPs.

Lending & Mortgages CFPB / ECB Standard

Standard Fixed-Rate Amortization Formula

M = P • [ r(1 + r)n ] / [ (1 + r)n - 1 ]

Where M is the monthly payment, P is the principal loan balance, r is the periodic monthly interest rate (annual APR divided by 12), and n is the total number of amortization months (360 for 30-year mortgages, 180 for 15-year loans). In the initial years, monthly payments predominantly service accrued interest; principal equity accumulation accelerates exponentially in the latter half of the loan term.

Regional Nuance: The US market heavily features 30-year fixed rate loans guaranteed by Fannie Mae/Freddie Mac, whereas the UK and Eurozone predominantly utilize 2 to 5-year fixed resets or Euribor/SONIA-indexed tracker mortgages.
Wealth & Capital Exponential Asset Growth

Discrete & Continuous Compounding Equation

A = P • (1 + r / n)nt

Where A is the final future valuation, P is the initial principal investment, r is the nominal annual rate of return, n is the annual compounding frequency (12 for monthly, 365 for daily), and t is time in years. Albert Einstein famously described compound interest as the eighth wonder of the world: reinvested earnings generate recursive yields that accelerate over multi-decade horizons.

Empirical Benchmark: Historically, broad market equity indices (such as the US S&P 500 and European MSCI World) have produced long-term annualized nominal returns between 8% and 10%.

Derived from the landmark 1998 Trinity University financial study, the 4% Rule asserts that an investor with a balanced equity-bond portfolio (typically 50% to 75% stocks) can withdraw 4% of their initial portfolio balance in year one, adjusted annually for inflation, with an overwhelming 95%+ probability that their capital will sustain a 30-year retirement without depletion.

Early Retirement Note: For early retirees pursuing horizons of 40 to 50 years, modern academic consensus suggests adopting a conservative 3.25% to 3.5% withdrawal rate ($30x to 33x annual expenditures).
Macroeconomics & Inflation Fisher Equation

Real Rate of Return (Purchasing Power Yield)

1 + rreal = (1 + rnominal) / (1 + i)

Nominal yields mask purchasing power degradation. If an investor earns 7% nominal return on high-yield bonds while consumer price inflation (CPI) averages 3.5%, the authentic economic growth is approximately 3.38% per year. Over a 20-year horizon, failing to outpace inflation cuts the effective spending power of idle cash by more than 45%.

Hedging Assets: Real estate, dividend-growth equities, and inflation-protected securities (US TIPS, UK Index-Linked Gilts) serve as structural inflation shields.

Q: What is the monthly payment on a $400,000 mortgage at 6.5% interest?

On a 30-year fixed loan with 20% down ($80,000 down payment, $320,000 loan balance), your monthly principal and interest payment is $2,022.62. Over 30 years, total payments equal $728,144, representing $408,144 in lifetime interest outlay.

Q: How much do I need to retire under the FIRE 4% rule?

Under the 4% rule, your target retirement corpus equals your expected annual living expenditures multiplied by 25. For example, if your household spends $60,000 annually, your baseline financial independence number is $1,500,000 invested across diversified assets.

Q: Which is better: Debt Snowball or Debt Avalanche?

The Debt Avalanche method mathematically saves the most money by eliminating highest-APR balances first (e.g., 24% credit cards before 5% student debt). The Debt Snowball method pays smallest balances first, creating psychological momentum that helps individuals stick to repayment budgets.

Q: Is it better to rent or buy a home in 2026?

Renting is generally more cost-effective if contractual rent is significantly lower than mortgage principal, interest, taxes, and insurance (PITI), provided you invest the down payment difference into index funds. Buying builds equity over 7+ year horizons and hedges against long-term rental inflation.

Q: How is commercial property Cap Rate computed?

Capitalization Rate equals Net Operating Income (Gross Rents minus operating expenses and vacancy reserve) divided by the property asset acquisition valuation: Cap Rate = NOI / Property Value. A higher cap rate reflects higher cash flow yield, typically accompanied by higher operational risk.
